Smith River, California

Jedediah Smith Redwoods State Park

Jedediah Smith Redwoods State Park is, for many who have visited all five parks in the complex, the most moving. Named for Jedediah Strong Smith — the first American explorer to reach California overland — the park sits in the far north of the redwood region, just south of the Oregon border, centered on the incomparably beautiful Smith River.

The Smith River is the longest undammed river in California. Its water is a startling, vivid turquoise in summer, fed by snowmelt and spring seeps flowing through hundreds of miles of protected wilderness. The combination of this emerald river running through the densest concentration of old-growth redwood forest anywhere on Earth creates an environment of extraordinary beauty and meditative calm.

The Stout Memorial Grove — a flat, alluvial stand of trees beside the river — is by many accounts the single most beautiful spot in all the California redwood parks. Trees here reach 340 feet and 2,100 years in age, growing in a grove that feels less like a park and more like a natural temple.

What to See & Do

Key Attractions

Stout Memorial Grove

The crown jewel of the park — a flat grove of enormous redwoods beside the Smith River. The short trail here delivers maximum impact with minimum effort. Visit at sunrise for light that seems almost supernatural.

Howland Hill Road

A 10-mile unpaved road through the densest old-growth grove accessible by vehicle in the park system. Cars and SUVs only — no RVs. Pure forest immersion with no crowds.

Smith River Swimming

In summer, the turquoise pools of the Smith River offer some of the finest freshwater swimming in California. Cold, crystal-clear, pristine, and surrounded by old-growth forest.

Simpson-Reed Grove

Accessible directly off US-199, this grove features an easy interpretive loop through old-growth and is an ideal first stop for first-time visitors arriving from Crescent City.

Jedediah Smith Campground

86 sites along the brilliant turquoise Smith River, with hot showers, swimming access, and trail connections. Beloved by swimmers, anglers, and kayakers. Open May through September.

Boy Scout Tree Trail

A 5.4-mile round-trip through old-growth to one of the largest known coast redwoods — the Boy Scout Tree, named for the scout troop that discovered it. One of the park's finest quiet hikes.

Protect the Smith River

The Smith River is one of California's last pristine river ecosystems. No soap, sunscreen, or food residue in the water — even biodegradable products affect the ecosystem. Pack it in, pack it out.

Insider Tips

  • • Arrive at Stout Grove at sunrise — it will move you
  • • River swimming peaks July–August when water warms
  • • Howland Hill Road is best driven slowly — stop often
  • • Campground books out months ahead in summer
  • • Fall steelhead runs begin in October
