Humboldt Redwoods State Park

Humboldt Redwoods State Park is the largest of the California redwood state parks, protecting over 53,000 acres including the Rockefeller Forest — at 10,000 acres, the world's largest remaining old-growth coast redwood forest. It sits roughly 45 miles south of the main Redwood National Park cluster, accessible from US-101 via the Avenue of the Giants: a 32-mile alternative scenic drive that passes directly beneath old-growth trees.

Where the northern parks feel intimate and cathedral-quiet, Humboldt Redwoods has a different quality — vast, almost oceanic in scale. The Bull Creek Flats in particular are staggering in their proportions, where trees over 350 feet tall grow in a continuous grove stretching for miles. Walking here requires deliberately recalibrating your sense of scale; most visitors spend several minutes simply standing still, feeling disoriented by the immensity above them.

The park receives fewer international visitors than its northern counterparts, which makes it in many ways a better destination for travelers willing to do a little research. Less crowded, equally magnificent, and home to some of the longest and most rewarding backcountry routes in the entire redwood parks system.

What to See & Do

Key Attractions

Avenue of the Giants

A 32-mile scenic drive along the South Fork Eel River beneath old-growth canopy — the most accessible redwood experience in California. Simply pull off anywhere and walk into the trees.

Rockefeller Forest

The world's largest old-growth redwood forest — 10,000 unlogged acres. The Flat Iron Tree here is among the largest redwoods in existence. Enter from the Bull Creek Flats trailhead.

Founders Grove

A short, beautiful loop honoring the founders of Save-the-Redwoods League. Features the fallen Dyerville Giant — once the world's tallest known tree at 362 feet, toppled in 1991.

Bull Creek Flats

Miles of trail through continuous old-growth along Bull Creek, with trees reaching over 350 feet. This is one of the most spectacular hiking corridors in California. Plan a full day.

Burlington Campground

The main campground, 2 miles south of Weott, with easy access to the Avenue of Giants and Founders Grove. Albee Creek and Cuneo Creek campgrounds offer more secluded forest settings.

South Fork Eel River

The river that flows through the park offers swimming holes, fishing, and kayaking. In summer, gravel bars along the Avenue of Giants make perfect picnic spots alongside cold, clear water.

Leave No Trace

The Rockefeller Forest is one of the most significant ecological sites on Earth. Stay on designated trails, do not cut switchbacks, and pack out everything you bring in. These ancient trees took 2,000 years to grow and cannot be replaced.

Getting Here

Insider Tips

  • • Avenue of Giants is drivable in 1 hour; walk it in 2–3 days
  • • Rockefeller Forest: park at Bull Creek Flats, walk north
  • • Founders Grove is a perfect 20-minute stop off the highway
  • • Fall brings golden light and almost no crowds
  • • Combine with a night in the Victorian town of Ferndale nearby
