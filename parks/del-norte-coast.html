Del Norte Coast Redwoods State Park

Del Norte Coast Redwoods State Park occupies a dramatic stretch of California's northernmost coast, where the redwood forest tumbles down from inland ridges and meets the Pacific in a tangle of sea stacks, hidden beaches, and fog-draped headlands. The park protects both old-growth redwood forest and wild coastal terrain that few visitors ever see, simply because access is rugged and hiking is challenging.

This is the northernmost and wildest corner of the California redwood parks, with terrain more reminiscent of the Pacific Northwest than the California most people picture. Fog is common, rainfall heavy, and the coastline genuinely dramatic. The 6,400 protected acres feel far larger than they are, because the terrain is so varied and the access so limited.

At its heart is the Damnation Creek Trail — one of the finest and most challenging hikes in the California state parks system, descending 1,100 feet from an old-growth grove to a completely secluded Pacific beach. Alongside it, Mill Creek Campground offers one of the more atmospheric camping experiences in the region.

What to See & Do

Key Attractions

Damnation Creek Trail

The park's signature hike — a strenuous 4.4-mile round trip descending 1,100 feet from old-growth forest to a completely hidden Pacific beach. Trekking poles strongly recommended.

Explore Trail →

Mill Creek Campground

A 145-site campground set in a forested canyon, ideal for exploring both the coastal trails and the park's quieter inland groves. The most atmospheric campground in the northern parks cluster.

Crescent Beach Overlook

A dramatic viewpoint just south of Crescent City with sweeping panoramic views of the Pacific coast and forested headlands. Excellent for whale watching from December through April.

Enderts Beach

A less-visited beach near Crescent City that rewards the short walk with tide pools, dramatic sea stacks, and an entirely undisturbed stretch of Northern California coast.

Coastal Trail

The long-distance Coastal Trail runs through the park, offering multi-day hiking through old-growth and along the cliffs above the Pacific. Backpacker campsites available with permit.

Gray Whale Watching

December through April, gray whales migrate along this coast in impressive numbers. The headlands above the Damnation Creek trailhead and Crescent Beach Overlook are prime viewing spots.

Coastal Safety

Never turn your back on the ocean along this coast. Sneaker waves — large, unexpected waves — occasionally surge up the beach without warning, even on calm days. The surf at the Damnation Creek beach is particularly powerful; do not swim or wade.

  • • Dec–Apr: binoculars for whale watching from headlands
  • • Mill Creek CG is quieter than Prairie Creek — great escape
  • • Tide pool visits: check tide charts and arrive at low tide
