Redwood National Park

Redwood National Park is the federal heart of the greater redwood parks complex — a patchwork of national and state lands protecting the last remaining old-growth coast redwood forests on Earth. Established in 1968 and designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site and International Biosphere Reserve, the park encompasses 58,000 federal acres stretching from the banks of Redwood Creek north to the Oregon border.

The park is not a single contiguous wilderness but a mosaic of old-growth groves, recovering second-growth forest, and wild Pacific coastline. Its defining features are the trees themselves — coast redwoods that regularly exceed 300 feet in height and 2,000 years in age. Somewhere in this park grows Hyperion, the tallest known tree on Earth at 380.3 feet, discovered in 2006 and deliberately kept secret to protect it from overuse.

Visiting Redwood National Park is less like visiting a conventional park and more like stepping into deep biological time. The forest these trees anchor has existed, in essentially its current form, for millions of years.

What to See & Do

Key Attractions

Tall Trees Grove

The park's crown jewel — a flat, alluvial grove of record-breaking trees beside Redwood Creek. Some trees here exceed 350 feet. Free permit required from Kuchel Visitor Center.

Explore Trail →

Lady Bird Johnson Grove

A beautiful 1-mile loop through old-growth forest dedicated during the 1969 park ceremony. No permit required, accessible year-round, and suitable for all ages and abilities.

Klamath River Overlook

Where the Klamath River meets the Pacific — one of the finest coastal views in Northern California, with excellent whale and harbor seal watching from December through April.

Thomas H. Kuchel Visitor Center

The park's main visitor center in Orick. Issues Tall Trees Grove permits, offers ranger programs, trail maps, and up-to-date road and trail conditions. Open daily.

Coastal Drive

An 8-mile unpaved ridge road above the coast offering sweeping panoramic views of the Pacific and the forested headlands. Accessible to passenger vehicles in dry conditions.

Redwood Creek Trail

A 16-mile round-trip river corridor hike through recovering forest and old-growth groves to the Tall Trees area. Best as an overnight backpack trip with permits from the visitor center.

Leave No Trace

Stay on marked trails, pack out all waste, and respect wildlife. The Hyperion tree's location is kept secret because trampling by even well-meaning visitors destroys the ground around it. Please do not search for it off-trail.

Getting Here

  • From San Francisco5–6 hours north via US-101
  • From Los Angeles8–9 hours via I-5 to US-101
  • Nearest AirportArcata-Eureka (ACV), 1 hour south
  • HeadquartersCrescent City, CA 95531
  • Scenic RouteUS-101 coastal drive is far more scenic than I-5

Before You Go

  • • Download offline maps — no cell service in park
  • • Book campgrounds 2–3 months ahead in summer
  • • Bring rain gear — coastal fog and drizzle are common year-round
  • • Store all food in bear canisters at campsites
  • • Arrive at visitor center early for Tall Trees permits
