About the Forest

A Living Cathedral of Ancient Trees

The Redwood National and State Parks system stretches along California's stunning northern coast, protecting the last remaining old-growth coast redwood forests on Earth. These magnificent trees, the tallest living things on our planet, create a canopy so dense that sunlight filters through in golden shafts, casting an otherworldly glow on the forest floor below.

Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the redwood ecosystem supports extraordinary biodiversity: Roosevelt elk, black bears, marbled murrelets, steelhead trout, and hundreds of plant species call these forests home.

🌿 National Park since 1968

December – February

Heavy rainfall brings lush greenery and complete solitude. Some closures possible. Not recommended for beginners.

Hiking Trails

From leisurely forest strolls to dramatic backcountry routes, the redwood parks offer trails for every ability level.

Moderate

Tall Trees Grove Trail

📍 Redwood National Park 🥾 4.5 miles

Walk among the tallest trees on Earth. This iconic trail descends through pristine old-growth groves to Redwood Creek. Some trees here exceed 350 feet. Free permit required, pick up at the visitor center.

  • Free permit required
  • Most scenic redwood views
  • River access at trail end

Newton B. Drury Scenic Pkwy

📍 Prairie Creek Redwoods 🥾 3 miles

A peaceful loop through old-growth forest with stunning canopy views. Perfect for families and photographers. Roosevelt elk are frequently spotted grazing alongside this trail.

  • Family-friendly terrain
  • Excellent photography spots
  • Elk frequently spotted

Moderate

Fern Canyon Trail

📍 Prairie Creek Redwoods 🥾 1 mile loop

A 50-foot deep canyon blanketed in five-finger ferns that looks like it belongs in another era, it was a filming location for Jurassic Park 2. Connects to Gold Bluffs Beach.

  • 50-ft fern-covered canyon walls
  • Jurassic Park filming location
  • Gold Bluffs Beach access

Challenging

Damnation Creek Trail

📍 Del Norte Coast Redwoods 🥾 4.4 miles

Old-growth redwoods dramatically meeting the Pacific Ocean. Steep switchbacks descend to a secluded hidden beach. The rare combination of ancient forest and crashing surf makes this one of California's most dramatic hikes.

  • Secluded hidden beach at end
  • Old-growth meets the ocean
  • Whale watching (Dec–Apr)

Before You Hike

Best SeasonMay–October for all trails; winter may mean closures and muddy conditions
What to PackSturdy boots, 2+ liters water, layers, headlamp, and bear canister
PermitsTall Trees Grove requires a free permit from the Thomas H. Kuchel Visitor Center
WildlifeStay 75 ft from elk. Make noise on trails. Store food properly to avoid bear encounters.
ParkingArrive before 9am, popular trailheads fill by mid-morning in summer
Cell ServiceMinimal to none inside the parks. Download offline maps before you leave town.

Wildlife You Might Encounter

The redwood ecosystem is a biodiversity hotspot. Keep your eyes open and your distance respectful.

🦌

Roosevelt Elk

North America's largest elk. Regularly spotted at Elk Meadow near Prairie Creek. Stay 75+ feet away during rutting season (Sept–Nov).

🐻

Black Bear

Common throughout the parks. Use bear canisters for all food storage. Never approach or feed them. Most active at dawn and dusk.

🦦

River Otters

Often spotted along the Smith River and lagoons. Active and playful, they are a delight to watch from the riverbank.

🦅

Marbled Murrelet

A rare seabird that nests only in old-growth redwood forests. Their haunting call at dawn is one of the signature sounds of the forest.

🐟

Steelhead Trout

The pristine Smith River is California's premier steelhead stream. Watch them spawn in late fall and winter from riverside overlooks.

🌿

Redwood Sorrel

The brilliant green ground cover carpeting the forest floor. It folds its leaves on sunny days, a beautiful adaptation to the filtered canopy light.

Plan Your Visit

✈️

Getting There

  • By Air: Arcata-Eureka Airport (ACV), daily flights from SFO & LAX
  • From SF: 5–6 hours via US-101 North (scenic coastal route)
  • From LA: 8–9 hours via I-5 North to US-101
  • Route Tip: US-101 coast route is slower but far more scenic, worth every mile
🗺️

Key Parks to Visit

  • Redwood National Park: HQ in Crescent City; Tall Trees Grove
  • Prairie Creek Redwoods SP: Best for Fern Canyon & elk
  • Jedediah Smith Redwoods SP: Smith River & densest groves
  • Humboldt Redwoods SP: Avenue of the Giants scenic drive
💵

Fees & Costs

  • Park Entry: Redwood National Park is free; State Parks $8–12/vehicle/day
  • Annual Pass: CA State Parks Discovery Pass ($125) pays for itself fast
  • Budget Trip: Camping + cooking = $50–100/person/day
  • Full Experience: $150–300/person/day including lodging and dining
