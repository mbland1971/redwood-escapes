Jurassic Park 2Filming Location

Fern Canyon is one of those places that doesn't feel real. A narrow gorge carved by Home Creek over millennia, its walls rise 50 feet on both sides — covered floor to ceiling in five-finger ferns, chain ferns, sword ferns, and lady ferns so lush they look painted on. The light that reaches the canyon floor is green and diffused, almost bioluminescent. You'll understand immediately why Steven Spielberg chose this location for Jurassic Park: The Lost World — it looks exactly like what a primordial world should look like.

The trail requires several stream crossings — in spring and early summer these can be thigh-deep, while by late summer they shrink to ankle-high wades. Many visitors simply remove their shoes and carry them through. This minor inconvenience is worth every wet step.

Beyond the canyon itself, the trail connects to Gold Bluffs Beach — a long, wild stretch of Pacific coastline hemmed between ocean and redwood-topped bluffs where Roosevelt elk graze at the surf line at dawn.

Inside the Canyon

Once you enter Fern Canyon, the outside world disappears. The walls press close — in places the canyon is only 10 feet wide — and every inch of rock surface is covered with greenery. Pacific giant salamanders live in the stream. Dippers bob and plunge in the current. The air smells of cold water, green growth, and deep time.

The five-finger fern is the star of the show: delicate fronds arranged like an open hand, brilliant chartreuse against darker canyon walls. It thrives in exactly this habitat — constant moisture, filtered light, stable temperatures. Some of the individual fern colonies on these walls may be centuries old. The canyon formed where softer rock was eroded away by Home Creek, leaving harder basalt walls standing. In winter, waterfalls cascade directly down the canyon walls.

Gold Bluffs Beach Connection

Extend your visit by walking the short connector trail out to Gold Bluffs Beach after completing the canyon loop. The contrast is dramatic — you emerge from the cool green world of the canyon onto a wide, windswept Pacific beach with ochre sandstone bluffs rising behind you.

Roosevelt elk are frequently found grazing on the beach grass, seemingly unfazed by the surf. The beach itself is undeveloped and beautiful. There's a campground nearby (accessible by high-clearance vehicle) for those who want to spend a night between the forest and the sea — one of the most unique camping experiences anywhere in California.

Protect the Ferns

Please stay in the stream channel and avoid grabbing or stepping on the ferns covering the canyon walls. These are living ecosystems that take decades to establish, and physical damage is visible for years.

Trail Logistics

  • Access RoadDavison Road — high-clearance recommended, no RVs or trailers
  • Day Use Fee$12/vehicle (CA State Park)
  • Stream CrossingsPlan to get your feet wet; removable shoes helpful
  • Best SeasonMay–Oct; stream lowest Aug–Sept
  • DogsNot permitted in canyon
  • RestroomsAt trailhead parking area
  • Cell ServiceNone — download offline maps

Insider Tips

  • • Bring water shoes or sandals for stream crossings
  • • Visit early morning for the best light and solitude
  • • After wet weather, crossings can reach knee-deep
  • • Combine with Gold Bluffs Beach for a perfect full day
  • • Bring a tripod — the canyon light rewards long exposures
