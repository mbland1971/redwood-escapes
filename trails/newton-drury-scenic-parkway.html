The Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway is one of the most magical drives in the United States — a narrow two-lane road that winds for 10 miles through the heart of Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park, entirely beneath a cathedral canopy of old-growth coast redwoods. Named for Newton Bishop Drury, the conservationist who did more than perhaps anyone else to save California's redwood forests from logging in the early 20th century, this road feels like a tribute to what he helped preserve.

You can drive it, or better yet, walk it. The Big Tree Trail — a 1.3-mile loop from the Big Tree Wayside — is the most accessible old-growth experience in the parks. Here stands the aptly named "Big Tree," a coast redwood 21 feet in diameter and 304 feet tall. The trail is suitable for all ages and abilities, often completed at a slow, reverent pace.

The parkway also passes through Elk Prairie — a large open meadow where Roosevelt elk graze year-round. Seeing a bull elk in the morning mist, antlers rising above the fog with a cathedral of ancient trees behind him, is an image you'll carry for the rest of your life.

Big Tree Wayside

The Big Tree Wayside is the parkway's centerpiece stop. The "Big Tree" — 304 feet tall and nearly 2,000 years old — anchors a beautiful 1.3-mile loop through some of the park's finest old-growth. Interpretive signs explain redwood ecology: how the trees store water in their bark to resist fire, how they reproduce from both seed and sprout, and how a single old-growth grove stores more carbon per acre than any other forest type on Earth.

The forest floor here is carpeted in redwood sorrel — a bright green, shamrock-like plant that folds its leaves in bright sunlight, an extraordinary adaptation to life in perpetual shade. Trillium, wild ginger, and oxalis grow in the understory. The silence is profound, broken only by birdsong and the occasional creak of a massive trunk in the wind.

Elk Prairie & Wildlife Watching

Elk Prairie is one of the most reliable wildlife viewing spots in all of California. Roosevelt elk are here year-round, though herd size and activity levels vary by season. Summer brings bachelor herds of velvet-antlered bulls. Fall brings the rut — one of the most dramatic wildlife spectacles in North America, as bull elk bugle, spar, and compete for harems from September through November.

The protocol is simple: never approach within 75 feet. Elk look placid but are genuinely dangerous, particularly bulls during rut and cows with young calves. Park at designated pull-outs, use binoculars, and enjoy the show. Dawn and dusk offer the best light and the most activity. In morning fog, watching elk materialize out of the mist beneath old-growth trees is an otherworldly experience.

Respect the Elk

Elk weigh up to 1,100 pounds and can be unpredictable, especially during the fall rut and when cows have calves. Always maintain 75+ feet of distance, and never exit your vehicle in the meadow area when elk are present.

Insider Tips

  • • Drive slowly — elk cross without warning
  • • Morning visits (6–9am) nearly guarantee elk sightings
  • • Combine with Fern Canyon for a perfect full day
  • • In fog, the forest takes on a mystical, ethereal quality
  • • Fall rutting season (Sept–Oct) for bugling bulls
