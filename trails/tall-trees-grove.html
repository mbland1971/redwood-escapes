The Tall Trees Grove Trail is the most iconic hike in all of Redwood National Park — and arguably one of the most awe-inspiring hikes in the United States. This is where you come to stand beside the tallest known trees on Earth, some soaring beyond 350 feet into the coastal fog. The trail begins with a steady descent through vibrant second-growth forest before delivering you into a realm of primordial giants that have been growing since before the Roman Empire.

As you walk, the world changes scale. Sounds are muffled by bark so thick it could stop a small fire. Light doesn't reach the forest floor the way it does in ordinary woods — it filters, diffuses, and glows green through a canopy so high above you it's almost abstract. The silence is deep and particular. This is what old growth means.

The trail ends at Redwood Creek, a cold, clear stream that cuts through the grove. Here, in summer, you can wade in the shallows and look back up at trees that dwarf everything. Hyperion — discovered in 2006 at 380.3 feet — grows somewhere in this region, though its exact location is kept secret to protect it from trampling.

What to Expect on the Trail

The hike starts at a locked gate off Bald Hills Road, accessible only to permit holders who've picked up their free pass from the Thomas H. Kuchel Visitor Center in Orick. The road to the trailhead is unpaved and requires patience, but this access restriction keeps the grove from being overrun.

The first mile descends gently through a mix of young trees and bracken fern. Then the canopy closes. The trees grow larger. The air cools. By mile two, you're walking beside trunks that would take six adults holding hands to encircle. The trail is well-marked and easy to follow, but it's not a casual stroll — there's real elevation change on the return trip that catches many visitors off guard.

The Grove Itself

The heart of the trail is a flat alluvial terrace where the tallest trees concentrate near the creek. Coast redwoods grow to their maximum here — fed by groundwater, protected from wind, nurtured by centuries of organic debris. Some began growing before the Magna Carta was signed.

Look for enormous "widow makers" — fallen branches the size of full-grown oaks. Check the exposed root systems where erosion has revealed the surprisingly shallow but incredibly wide root networks. And don't miss the fire scars — blackened caves at the base of still-living trees documenting centuries of fire history without ever killing their hosts.

Leave No Trace

Stay on marked trails, pack out all waste, and respect wildlife by maintaining safe distances. These forests have survived 2,000+ years — help them survive the next 2,000.

Trail Logistics

  • TrailheadOff Bald Hills Road, Orick CA — gate locked, permit required
  • PermitFree — pick up at Kuchel Visitor Center, Orick
  • ParkingLimited — arrive before 9am in summer
  • Access RoadUnpaved, suitable for standard vehicles
  • DogsNot permitted on this trail
  • Cell ServiceNone — download offline maps before leaving
  • RestroomsAt trailhead only
  • WaterNone on trail — bring 2+ liters

Insider Tips

  • • Bring waterproof boots — the creek crossing can be ankle-deep
  • • Return trip climbs 600ft — budget extra time
  • • Weekday visits offer the most solitude
  • • Fall light (Sept–Oct) creates stunning photography conditions
  • • Pick up your permit early — they're limited and go fast in summer
